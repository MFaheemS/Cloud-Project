  • Error is non-monotonic; at 90–120 s the estimator is worse than the baseline because the unconstrained fit returns physically impossible negative asymptotes (−42.9 and −61.4 tok/s).
  • Oracle (same form fitted to the full run) reaches 3.8% mean error vs 10.5% deployed → 6.7 points recoverable.

H. Reproducibility problems found in the baseline's code

  1. No requirements file; scikit-learn, rouge_score, python-Levenshtein, nltk undocumented.
  2. signal.SIGALRM is Unix-only — crashes on Windows.
  3. Printing raw model output crashes under cp1252 when a model emits CJK text; one run died at 501/1000.
  4. Output opens in append mode, so a re-run silently concatenates. Produced a 1,501-line file that the evaluator scored without complaint.
  5. Energy measured with an external hardware meter on a Pi — not comparable to our NVML GPU sampling.
  6. Model tags unpinned, so exact weights cannot be guaranteed.

Items 2–4 mean the artifacts have never been run on Windows. Item 4 is the serious one: results that look valid and are silently wrong.

04 Research Gap — full specification

Target ~200 words. Four points, each tied to a measurement from §02, then one closing claim.

  1. No sustained-load dimension. Studies in 5.1–5.2 measure short runs. Our data: 43 % throughput loss over 15 minutes, and Q4's 21 % advantage over Q8 disappears entirely at equilibrium.
  2. Temperature carries no information during the loss. It saturates ~9× faster than throughput degrades (25 s vs 239 s), so systems in 5.3 that monitor temperature are blind through the window where performance is lost.
  3. Placement uses stale rates. Systems in 5.4 decide from a pre-measured service rate; ours is wrong by up to 75 %.
  4. A hardware ceiling in the baseline. Husom et al. could not host 1.5B above q4 on a 4 GB Pi, so their guidance for that size is incomplete — and the unmeasured region contains a 10.5-point accuracy gain.

Closing claim: no existing work measures how quantization level affects a device's ability to sustain performance, nor connects that to the placement decision.

06 Positioning against ThermE — do not skip

lu2026therme is the nearest work to ours and appeared recently. It must be cited in 5.3 and distinguished in the Research Gap, or a reviewer will raise it for us.

ThermEThis work
Predictsthermal headroom (temperature)sustained throughput (tok/s)
Whenat runtime, continuouslyonce, from a short cold-start window
Purposeschedule actions during servingcorrect the figure a benchmark publishes
Quantizationnot variedthe central variable
Placementnot addressedthe motivating decision
Platformedge SoCs, vLLMconsumer GPU, llama.cpp/GGUF

One sentence in 5.3 and one in the gap is enough. Suggested framing: ThermE manages headroom while serving; we ask whether the performance figure a configuration is advertised with is attainable at all under sustained load, and show the answer depends on quantization level.

ThermE also notes that vendor governors react only near throttling thresholds. That is adjacent to our finding that temperature saturates long before throughput stops falling. Acknowledge it rather than presenting ours as wholly new — our measurement is of a different quantity and is quantified (9×).
